## You Don't Lack Software. You Lack a Shared Context.

Most small and mid-sized manufacturers already run accounting software and have an ERP or plan to adopt one. Many also maintain ISO/GMP systems with hundreds of SOPs and forms, and their people have started using AI. Even so, the same situations keep coming back:

- SOPs exist, but each shift follows them a little differently.
- A key person leaves, and the department spends months relearning what they knew.
- Audit preparation takes weeks of chasing records.
- The ERP is live, but the numbers that matter are still in spreadsheets and email.
- Answering "Why did this batch go wrong?" takes three departments.
- People write faster with AI, but work doesn't move any faster.

These problems look separate, but most of them share one root cause. **Your company's information is spread across systems, files and people's heads. Nothing connects it into a shared understanding of how the business actually operates.**

<!-- section: os-analogy -->
## A Simple Analogy: The Operating System

Your computer runs Windows or macOS. Nobody buys a computer for its operating system. Without one, though, applications can't share files, and they can't tell who the user is or who is allowed to do what. The operating system is the **common environment** that lets everything work together.

Your company has applications too: ERP, accounting, quality systems, spreadsheets, documents, email, chat groups and AI tools. It also has hardware: people, machines and plants.

What's usually missing is the layer in between. In that layer, processes, permissions, documents, events and evidence are understood the same way by everyone.

Without that layer, **people become the operating system.** They remember, remind, forward, reconcile and re-explain. The business keeps running, but it depends on the memory and goodwill of a few individuals. When those people are busy, on leave or gone, everything slows down.

**OKELAS is that missing layer.**

<!-- section: what-is -->
## What Is OKELAS? {#what-is-okelas}

> **OKELAS is an Organization Knowledge Operating System for small and mid-sized manufacturers.** Many companies operate on scattered documents and personal experience. OKELAS helps them operate on processes, workflows, evidence and organizational knowledge instead.

Put simply, OKELAS connects links in a chain that today sit in different places:

| Link | What it means | Where it usually lives today |
|---|---|---|
| **Process** | How work *should* be done | SOP files, ISO manuals |
| **Workflow** | How work *actually* moves: who does it, which step it's at, who approves | Email, chat, verbal reminders |
| **Event** | What happened, when and where | Logs, shift reports, spreadsheets |
| **Evidence** | Records that prove the work was done right | Folders, paper, personal laptops |
| **Knowledge** | What the organization has learned and can reuse | Experienced people's heads |
| **Decision → Action** | Who decides, on what basis, and what happens next | Meetings, messages |

Once these links are connected, a company doesn't just *store* information. It *understands* that information and *uses* it in day-to-day operations. That is what organizational knowledge management looks like in practice on the factory floor.

**An illustrative example** *(hypothetical scenario, not a customer case)*: a production batch is found to be out of specification.

- **Without a shared operating layer:** QA opens a spreadsheet, calls the shift lead, hunts for inspection records and asks whoever handled the issue last time. The resolution ends up in an email that few people will ever find again.
- **With the OKELAS approach:** the event is recorded and linked to the batch, the process step, the current SOP version, the responsible person and the related records. Whoever handles it knows the next step and who must approve. The resolution becomes organizational knowledge for next time, rather than one person's memory.

## What OKELAS Is Not

To be clear, OKELAS is **not**:

- **An AI chatbot.** AI is one layer in OKELAS, not its center.
- **A document repository.** Storing documents is not the same as managing knowledge.
- **An ERP or an ERP replacement.** An ERP records transactions and operational data. OKELAS holds the understanding around those transactions: process, context, evidence and knowledge. **OKELAS doesn't compete with ERP. It helps companies prepare for their ERP, understand it and get more out of it.** → [Why readiness comes before ERP](https://www.okelas.com/en/why/why-before-erp)
- **A heavyweight enterprise system.** OKELAS is built for small and mid-sized manufacturers. You start with a specific problem and grow from there.

<!-- section: why-now-ai -->
## Why This Matters More in the Age of AI

Companies are starting to run into a paradox:

> **AI can help a 50-person company produce the paperwork of a 500-person company. That doesn't mean the 50-person company can handle the workload of 500.**

Reports, emails, proposals and documents get produced faster. But if processes, responsibilities, approvals and evidence stay fragmented, more information just means more to read, reconcile and search through.

The question is no longer *"How do we create information faster?"* It is *"Does the organization turn information into process, decisions and action?"*

That's why AI is not the center of OKELAS. AI works **inside the organization's context**: it understands the process, finds the right evidence, supports the workflow and leaves a trace that can be checked. A good answer isn't just correct. It can be **explained, backed by evidence and traced**.

Every organization starts from a different operational state. So OKELAS doesn't start by installing software. It starts with a **business readiness assessment across six pillars**.

Each assessment does two jobs:

- **Improve now:** it shows what you can fix immediately, whether or not you ever use OKELAS.

One important point: **the AI assessment isn't there to sell AI, and the ERP assessment isn't there to sell ERP.** Each assessment answers three questions: *where does the organization stand, what needs to change, and, if you use OKELAS, how should it be configured to reflect that organization?*

## From Assessment to Operating System: A Closed Loop

Assessment results don't end up in a report on a shelf. They become a model of the organization itself:

```text
Digitalization profile · Workflow profile · Knowledge profile
ERP readiness profile · AI readiness profile · Quality / eQMS profile
                              │
                              ▼
                     Organization Model
                              │
                              ▼
                Initial OKELAS Configuration
                              │
                              ▼
                     Implementation Plan
```

Once OKELAS is running, real operating data flows back into the assessments. Assessment is not a one-off exercise but a loop:

**Assess → Improve → Configure → Operate → Measure → Reassess**

That's why OKELAS isn't software installed into a company from the outside. It is **built from the way the company actually operates.**

## Components: Use What You Need, Configure as You Grow

No company needs everything on day one. Depending on maturity and needs, OKELAS can include:

- **OKELAS Core:** the foundation for processes, workflows, events, evidence, permissions and knowledge relationships across the organization.
- **Knowledge Graph:** the layer that links knowledge to people, processes, documents, events and decisions, so knowledge can be queried, verified and explained.
- **Copilot / Agent:** AI that works within the organization's context, with defined tasks, permissions and evidence. It is not a general-purpose chatbot.
- **ERPNext + ERP Knowledge Graph:** ERPNext serves as the transactional backbone where it fits. The ERP Knowledge Graph maps the ERP's structure, business rules and customizations to support implementation and day-to-day use.
- **ERP Sandbox:** a safe environment for testing changes and customizations before they touch the live ERP.

You can start from your most pressing problem:

- [Digitalize Operations](https://www.okelas.com/en/solutions/digitalize-operations): move records, forms and operating data off paper and out of loose files.
- [Standardize Processes](https://www.okelas.com/en/solutions/standardize-processes): make SOPs something people consistently follow.
- [Progressive eQMS](https://www.okelas.com/en/solutions/progressive-eqms): build an audit-ready quality system in stages instead of all at once.
- [ERP Readiness](https://www.okelas.com/en/solutions/erp-readiness): get processes, data and people ready before implementing an ERP.

<!-- section: principles -->
## Principles We Hold To

- **Smallest sufficient system.** We use the smallest system that solves the problem and don't build large systems just to showcase technology.
- **On-premise by default.** OKELAS is deployed on-premise by default, so your data and knowledge stay on your own infrastructure. You don't have to move data to the cloud or take on a stack of SaaS subscriptions. This matters especially for companies with sensitive data or compliance requirements.
- **Price / performance.** Costs must match the value delivered. Infrastructure, licensing, implementation, maintenance and AI costs are all sized for small and mid-sized companies.
- **Explain → Evidence → Trace.** Every answer should be explainable, backed by evidence and traceable. This matters especially under ISO and GMP.
- **One step at a time.** Move from digital documents to workflow, then to knowledge, then to AI. Each step must make economic sense on its own.
