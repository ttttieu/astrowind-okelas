---

> Most companies already have workflow. The question isn't "do you have one" — it's where that workflow sits on a longer journey, and which next step is actually worth investing in.
>
> The 10 questions below help you identify your organization's current position across 6 workflow maturity levels — from fully manual operations to workflow where AI participates as a fully governed participant. There are no "right" or "wrong" answers — only answers that accurately reflect how your operations actually run today.
>
> Time to complete: about 4–5 minutes.

## The 6-Level Scale

| Level | Name | Core trait | Related articles |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Manual** | Operations run on paper, email, or individual memory. No formal tracking system. | 5.1, 5.5 |
| 2 | **Digital** | Processes are digitized (electronic forms, digital signatures) but keep the same sequential logic as before. | 5.1, 5.2, 5.3 |
| 3 | **Automated** | Rule-based automation exists, some processes react to events, exceptions have a defined path. | 5.6, 5.8, 5.9 |
| 4 | **Context-aware** | Workflow references organizational knowledge (relationships, priorities, precedent) to flex rules appropriately, with full evidence. | 5.12 |
| 5 | **Intelligent** | AI participates in classification, routing, and precedent-based suggestions; the decision/execution boundary and the rule/reasoning split are clear. | 5.10, 5.11, 5.14, 5.15, 5.17 |
| 6 | **Agentic** | An AI agent ("AI employee") plans and carries out part of a task within the workflow, with its own identity, scoped authority, guardrails, and runtime monitoring. | 5.13, 5.16, 5.18 |

## 10 Questions

Each question has 6 options corresponding to the 6 levels (A = Manual, ..., F = Agentic). Pick the description closest to reality — answers don't need to align across questions, since different processes within the same organization can sit at different levels.

**Question 1 — Tracking Progress**
When an important request (an order, a complaint, an approval...) is in progress, how do you know where it stands?
- A. You have to ask the person handling it directly, or dig through email.
- B. You can check the software, but have to open each request individually to see its status.
- C. The system shows aggregated status, updated according to defined rules.
- D. The system shows status along with context (who's busy, current priority level).
- E. The system suggests a next step based on similar past cases.
- F. Part of the tracking/reporting work is handled and reported automatically by an AI agent.

**Question 2 — Process Scope**
How far is your most important process (say, complaint handling) actually modeled?
- A. No formal model — each case is handled ad hoc.
- B. There are digitized approval steps, but they cover only a short segment of the whole event.
- C. There's a basic end-to-end flow, crossing a few departments.
- D. A full end-to-end flow, with a process owner accountable for the whole thing.
- E. A full end-to-end flow with an AI-assisted classification/synthesis point in the middle.
- F. Part of the process is handled by an agent from detection to action, with a human checkpoint.

**Question 3 — Human Dependency**
If the person handling a key step were out for a week, what would happen?
- A. The process would nearly stop; nobody would know how to proceed.
- B. Someone else could handle it, but would waste time figuring it out from scratch.
- C. There's a clear backup rule, and someone else continues following the defined process.
- D. The system provides enough context for the substitute to decide quickly.
- E. Most routine work still runs thanks to AI support; only exceptions need a person.
- F. An AI employee handles the repetitive share of that role, significantly reducing individual dependency.

**Question 4 — Event Detection**
When an abnormal condition occurs (low inventory, an off-spec quality reading...), what happens first?
- A. Someone happens to notice, usually too late.
- B. There's a periodic report, but it only catches it after a delay.
- C. The system automatically alerts based on a defined threshold.
- D. The alert comes with context (severity, handling priority).
- E. The system also suggests a handling approach based on similar past cases.
- F. The system/agent initiates an initial handling action on its own, pending confirmation from someone with authority.

**Question 5 — Exception Handling**
When a case doesn't match the standard process, how does it get handled?
- A. It falls outside the system entirely — a phone call, a personal text.
- B. Someone handles it manually, with no record kept in the system.
- C. There's a dedicated exception branch in the process, and it gets recorded.
- D. The exception is evaluated based on specific context (relationships, priorities) before deciding.
- E. AI helps classify the severity of the exception before routing it to a person.
- F. The agent handles low-risk exceptions on its own based on precedent, escalating only genuinely novel ones.

**Question 6 — Current AI Use**
Where has your company used AI in operations, if at all?
- A. No AI use in operations yet.
- B. Standalone AI tools (writing, translation...) not tied to any workflow.
- C. Rule-based automation for a few repetitive steps.
- D. AI used to synthesize/prepare information, tied to a specific workflow.
- E. AI participates in classification, routing, or action suggestions within a workflow, with oversight.
- F. An AI agent participates in a workflow as a participant with its own identity, authority, and evidence trail.

**Question 7 — Decision Boundary**
When a proposal (from AI or a person) is made and an action follows, can your organization clearly answer: who proposed it, who authorized it, who executed it?
- A. No, things happen in a fairly mixed-up way.
- B. Yes, with some digging, but nothing is clearly documented.
- C. There's a clear approval process for most important decisions.
- D. Yes, and this boundary gets adjusted based on specific context when needed.
- E. Yes, even when AI is involved in proposing — AI has no authority to self-execute high-risk actions.
- F. There's a clear role model (propose/authorize/execute/record) that applies even to autonomous agents.

**Question 8 — Rule vs. Reasoning Split**
Has your organization deliberately classified which activities should be hard rules, and which need human/AI judgment?
- A. Never thought about it this way.
- B. There's an intuitive distinction, but it's not systematized.
- C. Fixed rules are clearly documented for the main processes.
- D. Rules are periodically reviewed and adjusted as organizational conditions change.
- E. There's a clear split of what's handed to AI reasoning, based on frequency/risk/criteria stability.
- F. There's a mechanism to track: once enough precedent accumulates, a "needs reasoning" activity gets converted into a new rule.

**Question 9 — Operating Capacity**
When workload increases, how does your company mainly respond?
- A. Hire more people, proportional to the increase.
- B. Try to work faster with the same resources, leading to overload.
- C. Optimize the process to remove redundant work before hiring more.
- D. Reassess resource allocation based on current priorities and business context.
- E. Hand part of the repetitive work to AI so people can focus on what needs judgment.
- F. An AI employee regularly handles part of the activity for one or more positions.

**Question 10 — Evidence and Accountability**
If you needed to explain an operational decision from 3 months ago for an audit or a customer, how long would that take?
- A. Not sure the full information could even be found.
- B. Possible, but it would take a lot of time digging through email/paper files.
- C. Retrievable from the system within a few hours.
- D. Retrievable within minutes, with full relevant context.
- E. Retrievable, including cases where AI assisted with a suggestion or classification.
- F. Fully retrievable, including actions an AI agent took on its own, with detailed runtime logs.

## Scoring Logic

- Each option A–F corresponds to a numeric value of 1–6.
- Total score = the average of the 10 questions' scores, rounded **down** (never up, since a serious weakness in one dimension shouldn't be masked by a high score elsewhere).
- **Additional guidance:** if 3 or more questions score A or B, display a caution note — "some core processes are still at a foundational level" — even if the average score is high, to avoid a misleading result suggesting the organization is ready for Agentic while gaps remain at the Manual/Digital layer.
- Average 1.0–1.9 → Level 1 (Manual); 2.0–2.9 → Level 2 (Digital); 3.0–3.9 → Level 3 (Automated); 4.0–4.9 → Level 4 (Context-aware); 5.0–5.9 → Level 5 (Intelligent); 6.0 → Level 6 (Agentic).

## Output by Level

### Level 1 — Manual
**Where you are:** Operations run mostly on people remembering, chasing, and handling things manually. This is a normal starting point — not a problem, as long as you know that's where you are.
**Main gap:** No formal tracking system, so speed and accuracy depend entirely on a handful of individuals.
**Next step:** Start digitizing your most important process — no complex technology needed, just one place to record official status.
**Read more:** Article 5.1 — "You Have Workflow. Why Is Work Still Moving Slowly?"

### Level 2 — Digital
**Where you are:** Processes have electronic forms and digital approvals, but are fundamentally the same paper process, converted as-is.
**Main gap:** Digitization improved recordkeeping, not decision speed or the ability to handle exceptions.
**Next step:** Review the process logic itself — which steps can be combined, removed, or run in parallel — before investing in more technology.
**Read more:** Article 5.2 — "Digitized Workflow Is Not the Same as Optimized Workflow"; Article 5.3 — "From Approval Workflow to End-to-End Workflow"

### Level 3 — Automated
**Where you are:** Automation handles repetitive tasks, some processes already react to events, and exceptions have a defined path instead of falling outside the system.
**Main gap:** Automation still runs on hard rules and hasn't yet referenced organizational context (relationships, priorities, precedent) when applying them.
**Next step:** Start building the organizational knowledge layer (relationships, precedent, priorities) that workflow can reference.
**Read more:** Article 5.8 — "Event-Driven Workflow"; Article 5.9 — "From Request/Approval to Event/Action"

### Level 4 — Context-aware
**Where you are:** Your workflow doesn't just follow rules correctly — it knows when a rule should flex, based on organizational understanding.
**Main gap:** Most "understanding context" still requires human interpretation; AI hasn't yet participated deeply in classification, routing, or suggestions.
**Next step:** Identify 1–2 high-volume, high-frequency processes to pilot AI in a classify/route/recommend role.
**Read more:** Article 5.12 — "Context-Aware Workflow"; Article 5.10 — "Where Can AI Participate in Workflow?"

### Level 5 — Intelligent
**Where you are:** AI already participates in your workflow in a structured way — classifying, routing, suggesting based on precedent — with a clear decision/execution boundary and a clear rule/reasoning split.
**Main gap:** AI is still mostly in a supporting (recommend) role, not yet autonomously planning and carrying out a chain of actions within a controlled boundary.
**Next step:** Evaluate one specific process to pilot controlled agentic workflow — with guardrails, a clear authority boundary, and runtime monitoring.
**Read more:** Article 5.14 — "AI Agent and Workflow: Who Decides, Who Executes?"; Article 5.16 — "From Workflow Automation to Agentic Workflow"

### Level 6 — Agentic
**Where you are:** Your organization already has at least one AI agent operating as a fully governed participant — with an identity, scoped authority, guardrails, and a runtime evidence trail.
**Main gap:** At this level, the question is no longer "should we deploy agentic" — it's expanding a proven model to other processes/positions in a controlled way, avoiding ungoverned "agent sprawl."
**Next step:** Build a shared governance framework for all AI participants/AI employees across the organization, instead of letting each department deploy independently.
**Read more:** Article 5.17 — "AI as a Workflow Participant"; Article 5.18 — "An AI Employee at Every Workflow Step"
