Starting on 14 October 2026, Ukraine's military began reporting an unusual problem: Starshield receivers stopped working when mounted on fast-moving objects. Ukrainian forces still had their soldiers, drones and artillery. But missions that depend on constantly updated information were affected almost immediately.

This article uses the episode as a lens for a question every operating executive should be able to answer: **your systems are still running, but are they still showing you what is actually happening, at the moment you need to see it?**

An organization can keep all its people, tools and procedures and still lose the ability to coordinate when the information flow connecting them breaks down. The real danger is rarely a system crash. It is a system that keeps running while its data quietly stops reflecting reality.

Many management systems work well at a slow cadence (the monthly review) but fail at the cadence of incidents (the shift, the hour).

## Four questions to test operational control

| Layer | Question for leadership |
|---|---|
| **1. See** | Do we know what is actually happening, fast enough to still act on it? |
| **2. Connect** | Does a deviation on the floor reach the people who need to deal with it? |
| **3. Direct** | When we see a problem, do we know what to do? |
| **4. Control** | Are actions carried out, recorded and checked for effectiveness? |

This is a discussion framework proposed by OKELAS, not a certified methodology.
