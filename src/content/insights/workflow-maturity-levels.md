Ask a CEO or COO to answer, right now, "what level is your company's workflow at" — most will answer with a gut feeling: "it's fine," "still slow," or "never really thought about it." Not because they don't care, but because there's no concrete scale to place themselves on.

Across the workflow article series on OKELAS, a 6-level picture has gradually emerged — from fully manual operations to workflow where AI participates as a fully governed participant. This article summarizes those 6 levels, with quick signs to recognize each one, giving you a starting point for self-assessment — before taking the full assessment.

---

## The 6 Maturity Levels

**1. Manual** — Operations run on paper, email, or individual memory. No formal tracking system.

**2. Digital** — Processes have electronic forms and digitized approvals, but are fundamentally the same paper process, converted as-is — same number of steps, same order, just a different medium.

**3. Automated** — Automation handles repetitive tasks, some processes react to events instead of waiting to be manually started, and exceptions have a defined path.

**4. Context-aware** — Workflow doesn't just follow rules correctly — it knows when a rule should flex, based on an understanding of the organization's relationships, priorities, and precedent.

**5. Intelligent** — AI participates in workflow in a structured way: classifying, routing, suggesting actions based on precedent, with a clear decision/execution boundary.

**6. Agentic** — An AI agent (or "AI employee") plans and carries out part of a task within the workflow, with its own identity, scoped authority, and runtime oversight.

This isn't a race to reach the top level as fast as possible — most companies don't need, and shouldn't try, to jump straight to Agentic. The goal is knowing precisely where you stand, so you pick the right next step.

## Recognizing Signs of Each Level

A quick, tool-free check: ask "if the person handling an important process were out for a week, what would happen?"

- If the answer is "everything would nearly stop, nobody would know how to proceed" — you're at **Manual**.
- If "someone else could handle it, but would waste time figuring it out from scratch" — you're at **Digital**.
- If "there's a clear backup rule, and someone else continues the defined process" — you're at **Automated**.
- If "the system provides enough context for the substitute to decide quickly" — you're at **Context-aware**.
- If "most routine work still runs thanks to AI support, only exceptions need a person" — you're at **Intelligent**.
- If "an AI employee handles the repetitive share of that role, significantly reducing individual dependency" — you're at **Agentic**.

An important note: different processes within the same company are usually at different levels. Purchasing might already be at Automated, while complaint handling is still at Digital. That's normal — not a sign of failure.

## The Next Step for Each Level

The sensible next step is always **the level immediately above**, not the top one:

- Manual → Digital: digitize your most important process — you just need one place to record official status.
- Digital → Automated: review the process logic itself before adding automation — which steps can be combined, removed, or run in parallel.
- Automated → Context-aware: start building the organizational knowledge layer (relationships, precedent, priorities) that workflow can reference.
- Context-aware → Intelligent: pilot AI in a classify/route/recommend role for 1–2 high-volume processes.
- Intelligent → Agentic: only consider this once the decision/execution boundary and the rule/reasoning split are genuinely clear.

Trying to skip levels — deploying an autonomous AI agent, say, while still heavily dependent on a few individuals at the Manual layer — usually creates more operational risk than value.
