Digitalization isn't a yes-or-no question.

No business is "completely undigitalized" and no business is "fully digitalized." Every organization sits somewhere on a continuous journey — and knowing where you are is the first step to deciding what comes next.

Here are 5 digitalization levels with specific recognition signals. Read through and identify where your business sits — or where it sits on each dimension, because not every department will be at the same level.

![Five digitalization levels for business — from level 1 (paper-based) to level 5 (AI-assisted operations)](~/assets/images/insights/lv-01-level-signs-en.svg)

---

## Level 1 — Paper-based operations

**Recognition signals:**

- Quality inspection records, production logs, delivery notes — all handwritten in notebooks or on printed forms.
- Retrieving information from three months ago means searching through multiple folders of physical records.
- Audit preparation takes 2–4 weeks because documents need to be located and photocopied.
- Information is transmitted primarily through direct conversation or phone calls.

**What to know:** This stage carries no stigma — many manufacturing SMEs are here. Problems emerge when the business wants to scale or faces increasing compliance requirements.

---

## Level 2 — Digital documents, scattered

**Recognition signals:**

- Documents are created in Word, Excel, PDF — but scattered across multiple folders, personal computers, email threads.
- "Where is that file?" and "which version is current?" are common questions.
- Excel is the primary tool for order tracking, quality control, production planning — even though other software exists.
- When an employee leaves, significant information is trapped in their computer or email account.

**What to know:** This is the level where many SMEs get stuck — they've spent money on software but operations still run on Excel because the software doesn't fit the actual workflow.

![Illustration of different digitalization levels across different business dimensions (illustrative example, not actual data)](~/assets/images/insights/lv-03-level2-loop-en.svg)

---

## Level 3 — Workflow through systems

**Recognition signals:**

- A document management system with version control exists — clear visibility into which documents are currently in effect.
- Approvals go through a system with a traceable history.
- Records are generated during actual operational execution, not compiled afterward.
- Audit preparation is significantly faster than at level 2.

However:
- Multiple systems still don't connect — ERP in one place, QMS in another, production data elsewhere.
- Answering questions that span multiple systems still requires manual consolidation.

---

## Level 4 — Integrated systems and analyzable data

**Recognition signals:**

- Data from critical systems is connected or can be automatically consolidated.
- The question "which product had the highest defect rate this month?" can be answered in minutes, not days.
- Operational decisions are based on dashboards and real data — not only on summarized reports from middle management.
- Trends can be tracked over time and problems detected earlier.

**What to know:** Few manufacturing SMEs genuinely reach this level — many businesses have ERP but still require manual consolidation because the ERP data isn't being used correctly.

---

## Level 5 — AI-assisted operations

**Recognition signals:**

- AI doesn't only respond to questions when asked — it proactively flags anomalies in operational data.
- AI participates in processes as a participant — collecting evidence, supporting analysis, creating drafts for authorized review.
- Organizational knowledge is developed sufficiently for AI to answer context-sensitive questions — not just search documents.

**What to know:** This is a level very few manufacturing SMEs have reached — and, more importantly, it cannot be reached without levels 3 and 4 as a solid foundation. AI on weak data produces weak results.

---

## One important point to remember

![Businesses typically operate at different levels across different dimensions — not all at the same level](~/assets/images/insights/lv-02-level-by-department-en.svg)

Most businesses aren't at the same level across all dimensions.

A business may have quality control at level 3 but production scheduling at level 1. Or finance at level 4 but warehouse management at level 2. This is normal — and it means "where are we?" needs to be asked dimension by dimension, not answered in a single sweep.

The next most meaningful step is usually the one that improves the weakest dimension creating the most risk — not the one that upgrades an already-strong dimension further.
