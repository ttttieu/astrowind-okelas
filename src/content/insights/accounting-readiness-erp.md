> **Executive Summary**
>
> - The finance and accounting module is typically the most delayed and least prepared component of ERP implementations.
> - The cause is not technical — it is that most organizations' existing accounting systems are not ready to connect to ERP.
> - Chart of accounts, cost allocation methodology, and opening balances are the three areas CFOs need to assess and standardize before implementation begins.
> - Getting this right determines whether financial reporting from ERP will actually be trusted and used for decisions after go-live — or whether finance will maintain a parallel set of spreadsheets indefinitely.

## Why Finance and Accounting Is Consistently the Most Delayed ERP Component

In many ERP projects, the finance and accounting module is treated as something to configure last — after procurement, inventory, and production modules are in place. The reasoning is usually that accounting seems simpler, less operationally complex, and CFOs or finance leads are often not brought into the project until late.

This is one of the most consistently repeated mistakes in ERP implementation.

Accounting is not a back-office module that can be configured independently at the end. It is the foundation that every operational transaction flows through. Every purchase order, every goods receipt, every sales invoice creates corresponding accounting entries. If the accounting system is not properly standardized and configured, the entire financial data output of ERP will be unreliable.

And when the CFO does not trust ERP's financial figures — even after go-live — they will maintain separate spreadsheets and reconciliation processes to validate the numbers. The goal of a single trusted source of financial truth is never achieved.

---

## What ERP Requires From Your Accounting System

Understanding why accounting readiness matters starts with knowing what ERP requires from the finance side.

**A clear and consistent Chart of Accounts (CoA).** The CoA is the complete list of accounts used to record every transaction in the business. ERP requires a CoA designed with enough detail to reflect operational reality, but not so complex that it becomes unmaintainable. More importantly: it must be applied consistently — the same type of transaction must be recorded in the same account, regardless of who is doing the bookkeeping.

**A defined cost allocation methodology.** For manufacturing businesses, production costs need to be allocated to products, departments, or cost centers using a consistent method. ERP supports multiple allocation approaches, but it needs to know which method the business uses and how it is applied — it cannot decide this on its own.

**Defined accounting periods.** When does the accounting month begin and end? Does the fiscal year align with the calendar year? Are there any special accounting periods (for example, a year-end adjustment period)? ERP needs this information to configure correctly.

**Clean, reconciled opening balances.** When ERP goes live, the system needs a starting point — the balances of all accounts at the cutover date. These balances must be accurate, reconciled against current books, and confirmed by someone with appropriate authority before being loaded into the system.

---

## Chart of Accounts: Why Getting This Right Matters

The Chart of Accounts is one of the most consequential long-term decisions in ERP financial configuration. A well-designed CoA from the start makes reporting, cost analysis, and audit compliance straightforward for years. A poorly designed one creates persistent problems.

In many organizations, the current Chart of Accounts has evolved organically over decades — accounts have been added when new transactions arose, but few have been removed. The result is a bloated CoA with hundreds of accounts, many of which are rarely used or inconsistently applied.

When moving to ERP, this is the moment to audit and clean up the CoA. Which accounts are actually used? Which ones could be consolidated? Which accounts lack clear ownership or definition? This cleanup work takes time — but it is far better to do it before ERP goes live than to burden the new system with the mistakes and inconsistencies of the old one.

## Self-Assessment: Accounting Readiness

Before starting an ERP implementation, finance should audit:

**Chart of Accounts:**
- Is the current CoA documented and clearly defined?
- How many accounts exist, and how many are actively used?
- Are there inconsistencies in how the same type of transaction is recorded across the business?
- Is there an owner/custodian for CoA changes?

**Cost Allocation:**
- How are production costs currently allocated to products or cost centers?
- Is the methodology documented?
- Is it applied consistently, or do different business units use different approaches?

**Opening Balances:**
- Are current GL balances reconciled to bank statements, subledgers, and internal records?
- When is the cutover date likely to be, and will GL be fully closed and reconciled by that date?
- Who has authority to approve opening balances for load into the new system?

**Accounting Processes:**
- What accounting processes need to be in place before ERP can reliably generate financial data? (examples: month-end close procedures, reconciliation processes, intercompany eliminations)
- Are these documented?

If the answer to most of these questions is "unclear" or "not documented," accounting readiness work needs to begin now — not during ERP implementation.
