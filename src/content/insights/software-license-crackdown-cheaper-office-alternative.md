Since May 2026, using unlicensed software in Vietnam has sat inside a government-directed intellectual property enforcement push. Many SME owners are running a familiar calculation: buy enough Office licenses for every employee, or find a cheaper office suite.

This article suggests adding one more question to that calculation. In the same year, AI tools began creating documents, spreadsheets and presentations on behalf of users, while people moved toward assigning work, checking it and approving the result. If the employee's role changes, what the company needs to buy may change with it.

A boundary first: this is **OKELAS's view for roughly the next 1 to 3 years**. It is not a data-backed forecast, and it does not conclude that Microsoft Office is going away.

> **Executive summary**
> - Coding tools show a pattern: AI produces the output, people review, edit and approve it. VS Code is not disappearing, but its role is shifting.
> - Word, Excel and PowerPoint could follow a similar path. Microsoft itself brought Copilot's agentic mode into all three in April 2026. This is a hypothesis to watch, not an outcome that has happened.
> - Vietnam's tighter software licensing enforcement is a separate event and does not prove Office is outdated. But compliance pressure and maturing AI arriving together make a reasonable moment to review how work gets done.
> - Moving from Office to a cheaper suite saves license fees but does not address the cost of manual work.
> - Another direction is a workspace that can take in, understand and apply documents to real work, rather than only helping each person write them.

## VS Code and Claude Code: a decade of habit that can change in months

On November 24, 2026, Vietnamese tech outlet GenK published a write-up of Gergely Orosz's interview (The Pragmatic Engineer) with Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella. According to that report, Microsoft spent more than a decade making VS Code the familiar tool of developers, while Nadella said that within about six months development environments had partly faded in the face of Claude Code. We rely on GenK's account and have not read the original interview.

The point is not that VS Code disappears. The same report says Microsoft is turning VS Code into a place to assign work to AI, manage AI sessions and check results. Developers still use the IDE to review AI-generated code, fix errors and compare versions, especially for parts touching money, authentication and databases.

![Two boxes joined by an arrow: the IDE used to be where code is written and is shifting toward where work is assigned to AI and results are checked.](~/assets/images/insights/software-license-crackdown-cheaper-office-alternative/wsp-01-ide-role-shift-en.svg)

*Figure 1. The role of coding tools is shifting, as reported by GenK.*

The numbers deserve a careful reading too. GenK cites the 2026 Stack Overflow survey of 13,193 respondents, in which 70.9% still use VS Code, down from 75.9% the year before. That is a decline, not a collapse. Orosz also described only an observation from a group of about 10 startup developers he knows.

The takeaway for a business is not "old tools will die." It is this: **when AI does the creating, a tool's value moves toward viewing, checking and controlling.** The open question is whether that happens to office tools.

## Word, Excel and PowerPoint could undergo a similar shift

On April 22, 2026, Microsoft announced that Copilot's agentic capabilities in Word, Excel and PowerPoint were generally available. According to Microsoft, Copilot can take multi-step actions directly in documents, workbooks and decks, instead of only suggesting steps. The experience is the default for Microsoft 365 Copilot and Microsoft 365 Premium. Microsoft also stresses that people should review changes and keep the ones they want.

In other words, the maker of Office is itself placing AI in the doer's seat and the human in the reviewer's. From there, a role shift could play out like this (OKELAS's picture, not something proven):

- **Word:** from where documents are written to where documents are read and refined.
- **Excel:** from data entry and formula writing to checking and analyzing results.
- **PowerPoint:** from designing each slide to polishing a deck that AI produced.

![Three cards: Word from writing to reviewing and refining, Excel from data entry to checking and analysis, PowerPoint from designing each slide to finishing; this is an OKELAS hypothesis.](~/assets/images/insights/software-license-crackdown-cheaper-office-alternative/wsp-02-office-role-hypothesis-en.svg)

*Figure 2. A hypothesis about the new role of the three Office apps (a view, not an outcome).*

Three points are worth keeping modest. First, Microsoft is not saying Office is declining; it is adapting. Second, Word and Excel formats remain the shared standard when dealing with customers and partners, and that will not change quickly. Third, the usage figures Microsoft published give only percentage changes, with no baseline or sample size, so they cannot support conclusions about effectiveness.

So we are not suggesting you predict Office will vanish. We suggest a more practical question: **if employees no longer create most content directly, does the company still need the same number of Office licenses?**

The answer depends on each company and contract. To answer it properly, measure first: how many employees actually draft documents every day, and how many mostly read, look things up, copy or compile?

## Licensing enforcement makes SMEs revisit Office costs

For years, SMEs could stay with Office because staff were used to it, customers and partners sent Microsoft formats, license cost was not large enough to force a change, and no alternative was convenient enough.

What has changed is enforcement. On May 5, 2026, the Prime Minister issued Official Dispatch 38/CĐ-TTg on combating and handling intellectual property infringement, directing authorities to run a nationwide campaign from May 7 to May 30, 2026 in a "no forbidden zones, no exceptions" spirit. Tuổi Trẻ reported that the Copyright Office also issued Official Letter 314/BQTG-QL&HTQT asking organizations, businesses and individuals not to use unlicensed computer programs. Enforcement continued after the peak period: according to the Ministry of Science and Technology, in August 2026 alone authorities found 1,134 cases showing signs of IP infringement, of which 1,117 were handled administratively. That figure covers all types of intellectual property, not software alone.

Two clarifications to avoid misreading. This is a government enforcement stance on software in general, not a change in Microsoft's policy. And the dispatch does not target Office specifically, although Office is among the most widespread desk software. *This is management analysis, not legal advice; specific penalties should be confirmed with legal counsel.*

Faced with this, owners typically weigh three paths:

1. Keep buying licenses for every employee.
2. Move to a lawful, lower-cost office suite.
3. Change how work is done so the company needs fewer traditional office applications.

![Three cards: buy licenses for everyone, move to a cheaper office suite, or change how work is done; the third needs ready processes and knowledge.](~/assets/images/insights/software-license-crackdown-cheaper-office-alternative/wsp-03-three-choices-en.svg)

*Figure 3. Three paths to weigh; changing the toolset alone does not address labor cost.*

The third is the one we want to emphasize.

If you only move from Microsoft Office to LibreOffice or OnlyOffice, the company keeps working the old way: still opening each document, copying data, writing reports and compiling by hand. That saves license fees but does not address labor cost or dependence on manual handling.

**Why buy a cheaper toolset just to keep a way of working whose role is already changing?**

A quick check before deciding, with questions for the executive team:

1. How many employees actually draft new documents each day, and how many mainly read, look things up or move data between files?
2. In a week, how many hours does a typical office employee spend compiling information from several documents into one report?
3. Which documents get created and then are hardly ever read again?
4. When you need a decision or a condition from an old document, how long does it take and who do you depend on?
5. If AI produced twice as many documents as today, could the company review and use them?
6. What would a cheaper office suite change in the answers above?

If most answers show more time going to reading, searching and compiling than to creating new content, the problem may not be the license price. See also [when AI creates too many documents](/en/insights/knowledge-management/ai-document-explosion).

One important caution: we do **not** claim that tighter licensing enforcement proves Office is outdated. The two events are different in nature. It is the combination of compliance pressure and maturing AI that creates a chance to review how work gets done.

## Don't just move to a free office suite: move to a new kind of workspace

We propose a way of framing the discussion: from a **Document Creation Workspace** to a **Document Consumption Workspace**. *This is a discussion framework proposed by OKELAS, not a certified methodology.*

"Consumption" here is more than reading documents. It covers seven capabilities: ingesting documents and data, understanding content and context, extracting key information, connecting it with other knowledge and data, analyzing and cross-checking, using results to carry out work, and generating output documents when needed.

For an SME owner, it can be put more simply: instead of paying for software so staff can create and edit thousands of documents, a company can use a workspace where AI reads, understands, compiles and applies all those documents to the work, while people review, approve and stay accountable.

![A chain of four steps: ingest, understand and extract, connect and analyze, execute and generate; people review, approve and stay accountable.](~/assets/images/insights/software-license-crackdown-cheaper-office-alternative/wsp-04-consumption-steps-en.svg)

*Figure 4. Seven capabilities of a knowledge-consuming workspace, grouped into four steps.*

One condition no tool can replace: AI applies documents well only when the company has clear processes, organized knowledge and results with evidence to check. Without them, a company just produces more documents and more checking. That is why we place this topic within [digital transformation for manufacturing SMEs](/en/insights/business-operations/digital-transformation-manufacturing-sme) rather than in an office-software comparison.

## OKELAS and the first step

OKELAS is not built to replace Word and Excel. OKELAS is an operational knowledge system: it moves a company from working through documents, people and personal experience to working through processes, workflow, evidence and organizational knowledge, guided by *explain, evidence and trace*. For manufacturing SMEs, the approach is the smallest system that can do the job, deployable on-premise when data must stay inside the company, and not requiring a stack of SaaS subscriptions.

So the message is not "swap Office for OKELAS." What is worth trying is to step out of a dependence on ways of working that center on Word and Excel, gradually, and at the pace of the company's own readiness.

A sensible first step is not a software migration but a clear view of where the company stands: where documents live, who creates them, who uses them, which processes are already clear. That is the purpose of the digitalization maturity assessment below.

## Don't invest more in old habits

A company does not have to keep a workspace designed for people to create every document, every spreadsheet and every presentation by hand.

When AI can read, understand, compile and generate content, what a company needs is not just a cheaper Office suite. It needs a next-generation workspace where documents are digested into knowledge, knowledge is turned into action, and the results of work are recorded and controlled.

**This is not only a chance to save on license fees. It is a moment to review how the company works.**
