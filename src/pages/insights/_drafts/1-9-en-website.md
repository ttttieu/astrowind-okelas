# You Have ERP, But Your Team Still Runs on Excel. Here's Why.

Try a quick test.

Your organization has implemented ERP. The system is running. Employees have accounts. But if you walked into any department — accounting, sales, warehouse, production — and looked at the screens, what would you actually see?

If the answer is spreadsheets, email threads, and a few Word documents — you are not alone.

---

## Installed Is Not the Same as Adopted

This is the gap that few ERP projects acknowledge openly: *installed* and *adopted* are two entirely different states.

ERP installed means the software is running, data can be entered into it, and reports can be generated from it.

ERP adopted means the system actually reflects how the organization operates — not just where data gets entered after the real work has already been done somewhere else.

The distance between these two states is typically much wider than the project status labeled "successfully completed" suggests.

## Three Reasons ERP Does Not Stick

**1. The system was configured for a process that doesn't match operational reality**

When the system doesn't fit how work actually gets done, employees don't abandon the work — they abandon the system. A spreadsheet doesn't reject exception requests, doesn't enforce multi-step approval workflows, and doesn't throw validation errors when data is incomplete.

It's flexible in a way ERP cannot be — and for users operating under operational pressure, that flexibility has immediate value.

**2. There's no personal reason for end users to change**

People change behavior when they see a clear benefit from their own perspective — not from the company's perspective or the CEO's.

If ERP makes their job more complicated without visible personal benefit, and no one is genuinely requiring them to use it correctly, the path of least resistance is to continue working the old way.

**3. The data in ERP isn't reliable — and people know it**

This is the self-reinforcing loop that is hardest to break: when not enough people are entering data correctly, ERP data is inaccurate → other users don't trust what's in the system → they maintain their own spreadsheets for the information that matters → even less correct data ends up in ERP.

This loop does not resolve itself over time. Without active intervention, it typically stabilizes into a state where ERP coexists with an unofficial shadow system running on spreadsheets.

---

## Is This a Symptom or a Cause?

The more useful question is not "why are employees still using Excel?" — it is: *which parts of ERP is Excel replacing?*

If Excel is doing the work of the inventory module — the problem may be in how the module was configured, or in an inbound process that doesn't fit operational reality.

If Excel is doing the work of financial reporting — the problem may be that ERP data is not reliable enough to use in actual decisions.

If Excel is being used to store information the system has no place to capture — the problem may be that ERP scope doesn't cover enough of the real operation.

Identifying where Excel is substituting for ERP pinpoints where the actual problem is — and only then is it possible to address it in the right place.

---

## What to Do Next

If this describes your organization, there are two things to consider before taking action:

**Diagnose before prescribing.** Re-implementing ERP, switching software, or mandating system use without first resolving the root cause typically generates friction without solving the problem.

**Look at both dimensions: the system and the organization.** ERP not reflecting real processes is a system problem. Employees not having a reason to change is an organizational problem. In most cases, both exist simultaneously.
